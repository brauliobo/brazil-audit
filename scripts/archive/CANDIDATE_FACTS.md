# Historical electoral result facts

The browser reads source facts and runs its own SQL. These tables contain aggregate counts and public nomination records; row counts are not numbers of voters, people, or polling sections.

- `election_vote_rollups` sums source observations by election, office, candidate/list and UF. Local elections retain their municipality and nomination identity before any aggregation. It is the small default source.
- `election_votes` retains the published municipality/zone grain and destination. It is loaded separately for a narrower geographic query.
- `election_candidates` preserves names, numbers, party/list labels and reported outcomes attached to result records. A party list is not a candidate.
- `candidate_attributes` preserves public nominations, including nominations without reported valid votes, with official outcomes, categorical gender/race/education/occupation and age bands. Missing result records remain missing, rather than becoming zero votes. CPF, voter-registration numbers, contact details and exact birth dates are excluded.

The election identity includes the official event code, reported round and actual election date where supplied. Historical undated events retain the summary registry's unknown round instead of being relabelled as modern first rounds. Nomination keys include the election, office and electoral jurisdiction. Known national, state and local offices have separate jurisdictions. A municipality in an old `SG_UE` reporting field does not turn a gubernatorial nomination into a municipal nomination. Historical unknown office meanings retain their reported labels. Official candidate sequence identifiers are preserved; missing legacy sequences use an explicit composite of the published number, name and party context. No fuzzy person matching is performed. The 898 councillor observations reported only at UF level in 1934 retain distinct source-record identities in `candidate_identity`, with `context_unresolved`; a missing municipal jurisdiction is never reconstructed from a name or a modern office code.

The complete national `BRASIL` member is selected when present. Coverage by office, UF and reported round is audited against the territorial exports before publication; the national member includes all offices, not only president. Otherwise the archive's territorial/national members are retained. Member selections and source ZIP hashes are recorded in `memberAudits`. The 1998 native presidential data are in `BRASIL`, and the native `QT_VOTOS_NOMINAIS_VALIDOS` column has a different meaning from the concurrently reported `QT_VOTOS_NOMINAIS` column. Column presence establishes the source contract; a zero is never used as a signal to substitute another column. A real-source check reproduces Acre's 193,081 valid presidential votes and 195,645 valid gubernatorial votes in 1998.

A `destination` of `valid` denotes the explicitly reported valid nominal/list count. Historical reported totals keep their reported destination. Per-section registered votes are a separate namespace and may include votes without a corresponding valid aggregate nomination. Those two quantities must not be equated or silently balanced.

Every transported part has explicit election IDs, office keys, source years, states, cities, grain and source role. Parts are deterministic gzip streams smaller than 20 MiB. `source_part` is the exact database reload boundary, so replacing one cached part cannot delete a sibling. Large files are served from a pinned Git commit; each part also records its SHA-256, byte count and row count. Source ZIP URLs and SHA-256 hashes remain attached to the records.

Reproduce the normalizations with `candidate_facts.py`, `rollup_candidate_facts.py`, `candidate_attributes.py` and `pack_candidate_facts.py`, passing the downloaded official archive directory with `--root`. The input files are selected by their catalogued URL hashes. The collection catalog and source manifests describe their provenance. `test/archive/test_candidate_facts.py` covers jurisdiction, event identity, native count columns, member selection and the attribute allowlist. `test/election-results.test.js` checks the runtime SQL's hierarchy, population and candidate isolation.
